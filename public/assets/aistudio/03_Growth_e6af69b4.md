# HERBIZ AI Knowledge File 03 — Business Growth

**Purpose:** Help entrepreneurs assess business readiness, improve operations, retain customers, plan capacity and expand carefully.

## 1. Growth should be sustainable

Growth can mean higher sales, better margins, more repeat customers, improved quality, wider reach or more reliable operations. More orders are not always better if the business cannot fulfil them profitably.

Before expanding, review:
- Customer demand and repeat purchases.
- Profit contribution by product or service.
- Cash available and upcoming obligations.
- Production or service capacity.
- Supplier reliability.
- Quality and delivery performance.
- Owner and team workload.
- Legal, safety and compliance requirements.

## 2. Set clear business goals

Use a goal that is specific, measurable, realistic, relevant and time-bound.

Example: “Increase repeat orders from the current measured baseline over the next 90 days” is more useful than “grow a lot.” Set the target only after checking current records. Do not invent a baseline.

For each goal, record:
- Starting value and date.
- Target value and deadline.
- Actions required.
- Person responsible.
- Resources or budget needed.
- Review date.
- Risks and fallback action.

## 3. Customer retention

Ways to improve retention:
- Deliver consistent quality.
- Set realistic delivery expectations.
- Make reordering easy.
- Offer useful after-sales support.
- Remember customer preferences only with appropriate consent and secure handling.
- Ask for feedback and act on recurring issues.
- Provide loyalty benefits only when the economics make sense.

Measure repeat purchases over a defined period. A one-time buyer is not automatically an unhappy customer; some products are naturally purchased infrequently.

## 4. Improve operations

Map the process from enquiry to delivery:
1. Enquiry received.
2. Customer requirements confirmed.
3. Price and delivery terms shared.
4. Order and payment status recorded.
5. Materials or service capacity checked.
6. Product prepared or service delivered.
7. Quality checked.
8. Delivery completed.
9. Feedback and follow-up recorded.

Look for repeated delays, rework, stock shortages and avoidable costs. Create simple checklists for tasks that must be performed consistently.

## 5. Inventory and capacity

For product businesses:
- Track opening stock, purchases, sales, returns, damage and closing stock.
- Identify fast-moving and slow-moving items.
- Set reorder points based on demand and supplier lead time.
- Avoid large purchases based only on optimistic sales assumptions.
- Check storage, expiry and quality requirements where relevant.

For service businesses:
- Estimate staff hours or owner hours available.
- Track appointment capacity and cancellations.
- Identify tasks that can be standardised.
- Avoid accepting more work than can be delivered reliably.

## 6. Expansion options

Possible routes include:
- Add a related product or service.
- Improve an existing product.
- Serve a nearby area.
- Build an online catalogue or ordering process.
- Partner with complementary businesses.
- Sell through suitable marketplaces.
- Explore institutional or wholesale customers.
- Improve production capacity.

Test one expansion idea on a small scale before making a large commitment. Estimate costs, customer demand, delivery requirements and the effect on cash flow.

## 7. Risk planning

Common business risks:
- Demand changes.
- Supplier disruption.
- Cash shortages.
- Customer concentration.
- Product quality problems.
- Data loss or account compromise.
- Delivery delays.
- Regulatory or tax changes.
- Owner illness or workload pressure.

For each important risk, record likelihood, possible impact, early warning sign, prevention step and backup plan. Do not imply that every risk can be eliminated.

## 8. A simple 30-day action plan

**Week 1 — Understand:** Review sales, expenses, customer feedback, stock and time spent on key tasks.

**Week 2 — Choose:** Select one priority problem. Define a measurable improvement goal and a low-cost test.

**Week 3 — Test:** Run the test with a limited number of customers or orders. Track costs, quality, time and feedback.

**Week 4 — Review:** Compare results with the baseline. Continue, adjust or stop the test based on evidence.

The plan should be adapted to the entrepreneur's capacity and circumstances.

## 9. HERBIZ AI growth diagnosis

Ask:
- What does the business sell?
- How long has it been operating?
- What is the main goal: more customers, higher margin, better operations, retention or expansion?
- What is the biggest current constraint?
- Which figures are available?
- What budget and time can be committed?

Then provide:
1. A short diagnosis based only on the information supplied.
2. The most important missing information.
3. Up to three practical actions, prioritised.
4. A metric for each action.
5. A review date and possible risk.

Do not label a business “failing” or “successful” based on one metric or a small sample.

## 10. Dataset rules

The 600 entrepreneur records in the HERBIZ AI project are fictional/sample records. Any growth patterns, sector comparisons, state comparisons or recommendations calculated from them are demonstrations only. Do not claim they prove actual growth rates or real regional performance in India. Clearly label sample charts and results.

- What should I do when I receive more orders than I can handle?
- How do I reduce stock wastage?
- Can you prepare a 30-day business improvement plan?
- Which business performance indicators should I monitor?

# HERBIZ AI Knowledge File 01 — Finance

**Purpose:** Help women entrepreneurs understand business money, record transactions, estimate profit, manage cash flow, set prices and make careful financial decisions.

## 1. Core financial terms

- **Sales revenue:** Money earned from selling products or services before subtracting expenses.
- **Cost of goods sold (COGS):** Direct cost of the products sold or services delivered. For a product business, this may include materials, production labour and packaging directly used.
- **Gross profit:** Revenue minus COGS.
- **Operating expenses:** Costs of running the business, such as rent, internet, advertising, transport, software and office supplies.
- **Net profit:** The amount left after all business expenses for the period. A simplified calculation is revenue minus COGS minus operating expenses and other applicable costs.
- **Cash flow:** Money actually entering and leaving the business. Profit and cash flow are not the same: a sale made on credit may create revenue before cash is received.
- **Working capital:** Funds needed for routine operations, including stock, supplier payments and short-term bills.
- **Break-even point:** The sales level at which total revenue equals total costs; there is no profit or loss at that point.

## 2. Useful formulas

Use the same time period and currency for every value in a calculation.

- Revenue = Units sold × Selling price per unit
- Gross profit = Revenue − COGS
- Net profit (simplified) = Revenue − COGS − Operating expenses
- Profit margin (%) = (Net profit ÷ Revenue) × 100, when revenue is greater than zero
- Unit contribution = Selling price per unit − Variable cost per unit
- Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ Unit contribution, when unit contribution is positive
- Closing cash balance = Opening cash balance + Cash received − Cash paid

**Example (illustrative only):** A small handmade-products business sells 100 items for ₹300 each. Revenue is ₹30,000. If direct costs are ₹15,000 and operating expenses are ₹8,000, simplified net profit is ₹7,000 and profit margin is about 23.3%. This is a teaching example, not a forecast or benchmark.

## 3. Budgeting

A basic monthly budget should include:
1. Expected sales by product or service.
2. Direct costs and stock purchases.
3. Fixed expenses such as rent or subscriptions.
4. Variable expenses such as delivery and packaging.
5. One-off purchases and planned equipment.
6. Expected customer payments and supplier due dates.
7. A cautious reserve for unexpected costs, if affordable.

Compare actual results with the budget at the end of each month. Record the difference, identify the reason and decide whether the next month's plan should change.

## 4. Cash-flow habits

- Update cash received and cash paid regularly.
- Track unpaid customer invoices and supplier bills separately.
- Avoid treating all bank balance as available profit.
- Plan stock purchases around realistic demand.
- Follow up politely on overdue payments.
- Keep personal and business transactions distinguishable.
- Review upcoming payments before committing to a new expense.

## 5. Pricing a product or service

A price should consider direct cost, a fair contribution to overheads, customer value, competitor alternatives and the business's positioning. Do not set a price only by copying a competitor.

A simple starting calculation:
1. Add direct material, labour, packaging and delivery costs that apply to one unit.
2. Estimate the share of monthly overheads that each unit must help cover.
3. Add a sustainable profit contribution.
4. Compare the resulting price with customer feedback and market alternatives.
5. Test carefully and record the effect on sales and margin.

Taxes, platform fees, discounts, returns and payment charges may affect the final amount. Seek a qualified accountant for tax treatment and complex pricing decisions.

## 6. Financial records to maintain

- Daily sales register: date, invoice/order reference, product, quantity, price, discount, payment status.
- Expense register: date, category, supplier, amount, payment method, receipt reference.
- Inventory register: opening stock, purchases, units sold, damaged/returned units, closing stock.
- Customer receivables: customer reference, invoice date, due date, amount and status.
- Supplier payables: supplier, bill date, due date, amount and status.
- Monthly summary: revenue, direct costs, gross profit, operating expenses, net result and cash movement.

Keep receipts and invoices. Follow applicable record-keeping and tax requirements.

## 7. Common financial warning signs

- Sales are increasing but cash is consistently running short.
- A product sells often but has very little contribution after direct costs.
- Too much money is tied up in slow-moving stock.
- Customers regularly pay late.
- Business and personal spending are mixed and cannot be reconciled.
- The business borrows to cover recurring losses without a recovery plan.

A warning sign is a reason to investigate, not proof of failure. Review the underlying records before making a decision.

## 8. HERBIZ AI response method

When a user asks a finance question:
1. Ask what kind of business they run and whether the question concerns sales, costs, pricing, profit, cash flow or budgeting.
2. Ask for the relevant period and currency if missing.
3. Show the formula and substitute the user's figures.
4. State assumptions and identify missing information.
5. Separate actual figures from estimates and examples.
6. Suggest a small, practical next action, such as updating an expense register or reviewing unit costs.
7. For tax, loans, investment, legal obligations or major financial decisions, recommend checking current official guidance and consulting a qualified professional.

## 9. Dataset and honesty rules

The HERBIZ AI demonstration dataset contains 600 fictional/sample entrepreneur records. Treat its values as simulated project data. Never describe them as verified Indian business statistics, actual survey results, actual loan approvals or audited financial statements. If showing a dashboard figure, label it as **sample dataset result**. Do not invent missing values or claim that a bank account, accounting system, or external analytics report is connected unless the application genuinely retrieves that information.

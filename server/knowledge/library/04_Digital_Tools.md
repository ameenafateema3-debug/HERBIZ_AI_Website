# HERBIZ AI Knowledge File 04 — Digital Tools

**Purpose:** Explain practical digital tools that can help a small business manage communication, payments, catalogues, sales records, marketing, security and analysis.

## 1. Start with the business need

Do not adopt a tool simply because it is popular. First identify the task:
- Communicating with customers.
- Showing products and prices.
- Receiving orders.
- Recording sales and expenses.
- Tracking inventory.
- Accepting digital payments.
- Creating marketing content.
- Analysing business performance.
- Backing up important records.

Choose tools that fit the business's budget, digital skills, device access and privacy needs.

## 2. Useful tool categories

### Spreadsheets
A spreadsheet can record sales, expenses, inventory, customer enquiries and monthly summaries. Use clear column headings, consistent dates, one transaction per row and separate sheets for different registers. Protect files with appropriate access controls and backups.

### Digital catalogues and messaging
A digital catalogue can show product names, descriptions, photographs, prices and availability. Messaging tools can help respond to enquiries and share order details. Obtain consent for promotional messages and respect opt-outs.

### E-commerce and online marketplaces
These can provide product listings, order management and access to customers. Before choosing a platform, compare fees, payment settlement timing, return rules, shipping arrangements, seller requirements and customer-support options. Terms can change; check the platform's current official documentation.

### Digital payments
Use a payment provider or bank-approved method appropriate to the business. Confirm payment in the official bank/provider app or statement; do not rely only on a screenshot or message sent by a customer. Never share OTPs, PINs, passwords or full card security details.

### Accounting and invoicing software
These tools may help create invoices, classify expenses and prepare reports. Check whether the software supports the business's local tax and invoicing requirements. Have a qualified accountant review tax treatment when needed.

### Analytics and dashboards
Spreadsheets and business-intelligence tools can summarise revenue, costs, orders, product mix and trends. A dashboard is only as reliable as its source data, definitions and refresh process.

## 3. A simple spreadsheet structure

Suggested sales columns:
- Date
- Order ID
- Product/service
- Quantity
- Unit price
- Discount
- Total sale
- Payment status
- Payment date
- Sales channel

Suggested expense columns:
- Date
- Expense category
- Description
- Supplier
- Amount
- Payment method
- Receipt reference

Suggested inventory columns:
- Item code
- Item name
- Opening quantity
- Quantity purchased
- Quantity sold
- Returns/damage
- Closing quantity
- Reorder note

Use consistent categories and do not store unnecessary personal customer information.

## 4. Digital safety checklist

- Use unique passwords and a password manager where appropriate.
- Turn on multi-factor authentication when available.
- Keep devices, browsers and apps updated.
- Do not open unexpected attachments or payment links.
- Verify requests to change bank details using a trusted contact method.
- Limit staff access to only the information they need.
- Back up important business files regularly.
- Avoid sharing customer data in public groups or unapproved AI tools.
- Remove access when a staff member no longer needs it.
- Check privacy settings and data-retention terms of each service.

A legitimate bank or service provider should not need an OTP or password shared in a chat.

## 5. Using AI responsibly

AI can help draft product descriptions, organise ideas, summarise non-sensitive information and suggest questions to investigate. It can also make mistakes.

Good practice:
1. Do not upload confidential customer, payment, identity or business information unless the tool is approved for that data and its privacy terms are understood.
2. Verify factual claims, calculations, scheme details and legal information.
3. Do not let AI make final credit, hiring, eligibility or other high-impact decisions.
4. Tell users when content is generated or simulated if that distinction matters.
5. Keep a human responsible for important business decisions.

## 6. HERBIZ AI dashboard honesty rules

The HERBIZ AI dashboard displays figures calculated from user-entered data or clearly labelled sample previews. If the app shows a sample chart, mock-up, or manually entered figure, describe it accurately as a sample preview or user-entered estimate—not a live bank or accounting sync.

The chatbot must not claim:
- Data is automatically synchronised when no refresh integration exists.
- User records are saved permanently when persistence has not been implemented.
- A real payment, bank, marketplace or government account is connected when it is not.
- AI recommendations are guaranteed to increase revenue.

## 7. Selecting a tool

Compare tools using:
- Total cost, including subscriptions and transaction fees.
- Ease of use and language support.
- Device and internet requirements.
- Data export and backup options.
- Privacy and security features.
- Customer support.
- Ability to grow with the business.
- Compatibility with existing workflows.

Start with one small task and test it before moving all business records into a new system.
